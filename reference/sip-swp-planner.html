How the numbers are worked out
  • The lumpsum is invested in month 0 and the SIP at the start of each month it runs, split by your allocation (scaled to 100% over the holdings switched on). Each holding grows monthly at its net return: return before costs, adjusted by the Bear/Base/Bull scenario, minus the expense ratio. Growth options are assumed, so there are no dividends.
  • Withdrawals sell units first-in, first-out, so the oldest (usually most-taxed) units go first, as the tax rules require. Only the gain on the units sold is taxed, not the whole withdrawal.
  • Tax is worked out once a year on that year's gains. Equity: long-term after 12 months at the equity long-term rate on gains above the yearly exemption, short-term at the equity short-term rate. Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023: your slab rate, however long held. Other (hybrid, gold, international): long-term after 24 months at the other long-term rate with no exemption, short-term at your slab rate. Cess is added to the special rates; your slab rate already includes it. Losses within a category offset gains in that category only.
  • The ₹1.25 lakh equity exemption is shared across all your equity gains in a year, including REIT and listed-share gains. If you use it elsewhere, lower it here.
  • Tax is paid out of that year's withdrawals. In years with no withdrawals (for example while de-risking), it's paid by selling from the portfolio early the next year.
  • Fixed-amount withdrawals are set in today's money and step up once a year. A %-of-corpus withdrawal is recalculated at the start of each year. "Spread over N years" sells an equal share of what's left each month so the corpus reaches zero at the end of the period.
  • De-risking moves the equity share in a straight line from your starting mix to the target over the glide years, by rebalancing at the start of each year. Selling to rebalance realises gains and is taxed. It needs at least one non-equity holding.
  • The sustainable withdrawal is the largest fixed monthly amount, in today's money and following your yearly-increase setting, that lasts until the end of the horizon under these assumptions.
  • Returns on money (XIRR) treat SIPs as money in, after-tax withdrawals as money out, and the final corpus after exit tax as the last inflow.
  • Not modelled: exit loads (usually only in the first year), stamp duty on purchases (0.005%), TDS timing, surcharge on gains, and market ups and downs. Real markets don't return a smooth average; a crash just before withdrawals start hurts far more than the averages suggest.
